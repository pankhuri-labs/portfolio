# Rolling out a security platform to 10,000+ developers in four months

**Role:** Technology Product Manager, Application Security · **Organisation:** global manufacturing enterprise

## The situation

The company was replacing an incumbent SAST tool and a home-grown secret scanner. The existing contracts were ending, the old tools were noisy and unstable, and developers were frustrated with handoffs. The estate covered more than 55,000 repositories, including citizen developers building automations outside IT.

## What I did

- Defined the **evaluation criteria** and ran a time-boxed, phased pilot of secret scanning and code scanning.
- Picked and kept engaged a **pilot user group**, and guided a newly formed AppSec team to a **Go / No-Go recommendation** that leadership approved.
- Planned **rollout batches**, owned the KPIs, and built a **security champions network of about 100 people** across departments.
- Handled the hard trade-off: business units that couldn't yet move to the cloud got the tools on the on-premises instance, with an agreed path to migrate.

## The result

- Rolled out in **four months, a month before the old contracts ended**.
- **99% secret-scanning coverage** across all repositories, and **95% repository coverage** in the targeted departments.
- About **70% of findings remediated within SLA**.
- The method became the **template for later rollouts** of Defender and CrowdStrike.
- The rollout won the company's annual **CISO Award**.

## What this means for you

If you've bought a security tool and adoption has stalled, the problem is rarely the tool. It's the rollout plan, the ownership and the metrics. That's what my **tool evaluation** and **rollout playbook** services fix.
